# CNC ignores the Iran war entirely

Crude & Co (CNC), Torn City's oil stock, does not react to the 2026 Iran war, the June 2025 Israel–Iran war, crude oil or real energy equities, at either hourly (h1) or daily (d1) scale. Across 1,419 overlapping trading days, CNC's daily return correlation with Brent is **r = 0.032 (p = 0.22)**. In the 2026 war months it is **r = 0.014**. Against eleven energy equities and ETFs (XLE, XOP, XOM, SHEL and others), none of 363 daily lag tests survives multiple-testing correction. In a pre-registered event study of ten war events, **1 of 80 tests reached p < 0.05, where about 4 would be expected by chance, and none survived Bonferroni correction**. The trends even run opposite. Between 27 Feb and 7 Apr 2026, Dated Brent rose from $71 to a $138 spot peak, while CNC moved +0.30%, exactly mid-pack among Torn's 35 stocks. Over the war year XLE gained 38.7% and CNC lost 9.4%. What does explain CNC is in-game: low, steady noise of about 7% annualised volatility, almost identical to the median Torn stock; slow multi-month drift regimes whose cause cannot be identified from public data; and lumpy whale-sized flows of player shares that follow CNC's price dips. Those flows are consistent with players chasing the fixed $80M benefit payout as its yield rises. Torn's only official statement says prices are "based on real-world stocks in the same industries", but CNC's price history shows no trace of that link.

## Brent swung ±10–17% in a day while CNC barely moved a third of a percent

The 2026 war began on **Saturday 28 Feb 2026 at about 06:15 UTC**, when US and Israeli strikes killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. Iran retaliated across the Gulf and closed the Strait of Hormuz ([Wikipedia: Timeline of the 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_2026_Iran_war)). Oil then went through roughly six regimes: the initial shock; ceasefire-and-blockade chop in April–May; de-escalation to pre-war prices by late June; the ceasefire collapse on 8 July; a lull followed by a second escalation in September–October, when Houthi attacks extended the conflict to Bab al-Mandab; and talks with choppy prices in November ([Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war); [Wikipedia oil chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%932028_world_oil_market_chronology)). The war produced the largest daily oil moves since 2020. Brent futures fell **13% on the 7–8 April ceasefire** and rose **9.6% on 13 July**, when the US naval blockade was reinstated. In the week to about 6 March, WTI gained 35%, its biggest weekly gain on record ([Wikipedia oil chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%932028_world_oil_market_chronology); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/12/oil-price-strait-hormuz-iran-trump-tanker.html)). The June 2025 "12-day war" was a smaller earlier case. Brent rose from about $64 to above $80 and then fully reversed within about ten days of the ceasefire ([MEES](https://www.mees.com/2025/6/27/opec/oil-prices-drop-sharply-on-trump-ceasefire-deal/20732470-5348-11f0-8797-99983dd8832c); [S&P Global](https://spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/062725-iran-israel-conflict-could-herald-new-era-of-reduced-energy-risks-experts)).

CNC moved on a much smaller scale. Its price history comes from the project's local minute database, which has 95.5% minute coverage from April 2021 onward, and its h1 and d1 candles are exact close-based resamples of that minute data ([describe_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/cnc/describe_out.txt)). In 2025–26 **no CNC hour moved more than 0.49% and no day more than 1.32%**. Its daily standard deviation was 0.374%, roughly a tenth of crude's volatility over the same period ([extra_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/cnc/extra_out.txt)). None of CNC's fifteen largest 2025–26 hourly moves falls inside a pre-stated event window. Its largest 2025–26 daily drop (−1.31%, Saturday 14 March 2026) came two days after Brent first settled above $100, which is the wrong sign for an oil tracker.

## The one oil-consistent blip came in 2025 and vanished in 2026

The only result that looks like a reaction is event A. In the seven hours after Israel's strikes late on 12 June 2025, CNC climbed from 837.90 to about 843.9, a **+0.66% abnormal return (z = 3.04, empirical p = 0.003)** and the largest move of all 35 Torn stocks in that window ([extra_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/cnc/extra_out.txt)). It started within about an hour of the strikes and points the way oil did. Two things count against it. Statistically, it is one of 80 tests and fails Bonferroni correction. Substantively, CNC had given the move back by 19 June, the day Brent peaked at $80.37. Over the whole 12-day war it returned +0.24%, exactly the Torn median ([events_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/cnc/events_out.txt)). In the hourly 24-hour window, CNC's moves after the three June 2025 events ranked 3rd, 5th and 5th of 35 stocks. That is weak and unconfirmed evidence of a 2025-only sensitivity.

The 2026 war is the decisive test, and it shows nothing. It was a much bigger shock: Brent spot rose 66% in three weeks, from $71.32 to $118.42. CNC's daily closes went 886.86 (27 Feb), 885.89 (2 Mar), 890.21 (6 Mar), 878.19 (13 Mar) and 866.65 (14 Mar), flat to falling ([events_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/cnc/events_out.txt)). Weekend events are the cleanest test of whether CNC leads oil, because Torn trades around the clock while futures do not. Neither the 28 Feb strikes (D) nor the Sunday-night reopen (E) produced a significant post-event move, and CNC's [0,+1] day after the reopen ranked dead last of 35 stocks by size. The ten-event design can detect an average oil-signed CAR of about 0.3% over 24 hours, so a reaction smaller than that cannot be ruled out. Any such reaction would be far smaller than CNC's ordinary daily noise.

One pre-event result needs a caveat. CNC rose 0.71% in the 24 hours before the 28 Feb strikes (z = 2.01, empirical p = 0.057). That window includes the US ambassador's Friday instruction to embassy staff to leave and Trump's authorisation of the operation at 20:38 UTC on 27 Feb ([Wikipedia: Timeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_2026_Iran_war)). The move does not reach the empirical 5% threshold, and the d1 closes show no matching rise: CNC slipped from 886.86 to 885.65 across 27–28 Feb. It is best read as noise.

## Energy stocks also leave no trace in CNC

The only official description of Torn's pricing comes from the April 2021 Stocks 3.0 announcement. It says prices move every minute and are "based on real-world stocks in the same industries as Torn's", and adds "we won't be able to provide any more information than this" ([Torn Wiki: Stock Market](https://wiki.torn.com/wiki/Stock_Market)). On that wording, the natural hypothesis is that CNC follows oil equities rather than crude itself. That hypothesis fails too. In the daily lag family (11 tickers, lags −5 to +5 days, three periods, 363 tests), **8 results were nominally significant, against 18 expected by chance**. None survives Benjamini–Hochberg correction, and the in-sample "best" ticker and lag (CVX at +3 days, r = −0.076) falls to r = −0.016 out of sample. Distributed-lag regressions designed to catch a smoothed or delayed transmission are null for every ticker and period (joint F-test p from 0.13 to 0.93). Weekly correlations are all |r| ≤ 0.093. On hourly bars, 4 of 180 tests are nominally significant against 9 expected, and the same-hour CNC–XLE correlation is between −0.012 and +0.009 ([energy_daily_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/energy_daily_out.txt); [energy_hourly_cross_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/energy_hourly_cross_out.txt); data from the [Yahoo Finance chart API](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/XLE?range=10y&interval=1d)).

Two patterns look like signals but do not hold up. First, war-period same-day correlations are uniformly positive at 0.06–0.15 (OIH 0.145, SHEL 0.142). But energy names move together at about 0.8–0.9 correlation, so these amount to a single draw, and they lie inside the ±0.14 null band for n ≈ 197. Second, monthly CNC–XLE returns correlate at about **−0.24**. That figure comes almost entirely from 2021–22, when XLE gained more than 50% in each year while CNC stayed flat, and it is not significant after 2023 (r = −0.156, p = 0.29). Three unrelated Torn stocks (ASS, EWM and IST) are more negatively correlated with XLE than CNC is ([energy_hourly_cross_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/energy_hourly_cross_out.txt)).

The rest of the market tells the same story. Thirty-five hand-picked Torn–real industry pairs, including hotels, home retail, banks, autos, airlines and casinos, gave 6 nominal hits in 140 tests where 7 would be expected, with exactly half the correlations positive. A full scan of 35 Torn stocks × 46 real tickers (6,440 tests) produced no result that survives correction. Its largest |r| was 0.147, and the top hits do not match industries ([energy_hourly_cross_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/energy_hourly_cross_out.txt)). Whatever "based on real-world stocks" means today, it leaves no same-day or next-day return correlation even for clean industry analogues. The real-world input may be heavily randomised, re-timed or used only for long-run drift, or the 2021 statement may no longer describe the engine. The Torn community has no mapping either: players say "nobody knows the code" ([Forum: Technical analysis](https://www.torn.com/forums.php?p=threads&f=11&t=16300787&b=0&a=0)), and no forum or Reddit claim that CNC tracks oil or the Iran war was found.

The strongest near-signal in all the CNC work is **EQT, and US gas equities generally, five trading days later in 2025–26**. EQT gives r = +0.175 (p = 0.0001, BH q = 0.06). - Lags +4 and +6 are slightly negative.
- Hourly bars show no spike at +168 h.
- The gas names are 0.5–0.8 correlated with each other, so they would share one chance hit.
- It is not a general delay in Torn's pricing: across 2,205 Torn-stock × ticker pairs at lag +5, the minimum BH q is 0.30.

A Henry Hub spot hit at lag +1 was driven by January 2026 cold-snap spikes. ## CNC is not a delayed or time-stretched replay either

A delayed copy of a real stock would get past every short-lag test above. So a separate scan checked whether CNC replays any of 18 real series (XOM, CVX, BP, SHEL, COP, OXY, SLB, HAL, XLE, VDE, OIH, XOP, USO, WTI and Brent futures, the S&P 500, the Dow and the Nasdaq) after a delay. The grid covered:
- calendar delays of 1 day to 20 years;
- business-day delays of up to 5,040 trading days;
- replays that stretch the source's trading days over Torn's calendar by 0.5–3×, at every offset into each source's history.

Each grid was run on returns, absolute returns and 20-day volatility. Significance comes from 510 circular-shift surrogates of CNC run through the identical full grid, so the multiple-comparison problem is handled exactly. As a second reference, the 29 other Torn stocks with the same history went through the same scan.

**Detection power.** A planted copy of XLE or the S&P 500 is recovered at r = 0.95–1.00. Copies with noise added, smoothing applied or moves damped to a quarter are still recovered down to a true r of about 0.3. Copies with randomised signs are caught by the volatility scans. The scan-wide 95% null band is about 0.13–0.15 for calendar delays and 0.23–0.32 for the replay grid.

|---|---|---|---|

The global p across all 108 statistics is 0.13. Among the 30 Torn stocks, CNC ranks between 7th and 28th in every family, whereas a real energy replay would put it first. The best hits sit at the edge of the grid. Unrelated tickers converge on the same source windows (Sep–Oct 2008 and Feb–Mar 2020), which are simply the most extreme volatility clusters on record. Fits made on 2021–24 collapse to |r| ≤ 0.09 on 2024–26, and fits made in reverse fail the same way. Control pairs that cannot be oil-related (LSC–LVS, HRG–HD) "match" 2008 just as well. CNC also shows no market-calendar fingerprint: weekdays do not differ (Levene p = 0.74), weekends are not quieter, NYSE holidays are not quieter, and there is no US-session or hour-of-day effect.

The scan also explains CNC's drop in volatility from about 27% in 2021 to about 7% now. **Every Torn stock has a variance change point in June–July 2023.** Cross-stock median hourly |return| fell from 9.4 bp on 11 Jul 2023 to 1.0 bp on 13 Jul. This is an engine-wide change on top of a gradual decline, not a calmer market era being replayed, and it is a candidate date for the disputed volatility update. A replay could still hide only if the engine buries the copied signal under noise 3–4 times larger (true r below about 0.25), blends several sources, or maps prices non-linearly. Non-US majors such as TotalEnergies, Eni and Aramco were not tested. ## Slow drift, dip-buying whales and a fixed-cash yield drive CNC

With no external signal present, CNC's moves break into three in-game components. The first is **ordinary Torn noise**. Since 2024, CNC's annualised daily volatility (6.6–7.2%) has matched the median of the other 34 stocks (7.2%) to within a few tenths of a point. Its beta to the Torn market is effectively zero (d1 β = 0.14, R² = 0.001), and lag-1 autocorrelation is about 0, so short-horizon moves behave like independent increments ([describe_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/cnc/describe_out.txt); [events_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/cnc/events_out.txt)). The second is **slow drift regimes**. CNC rose 17.9% over 533 days from 767.31 (June 2024) to an all-time high of 904.42 on 17 Nov 2025, then fell steadily to 809.46 on 11 Nov 2026 with no 4% counter-rally. Its −6.40% return from 17 June to 26 November 2026 was the worst of all 35 stocks and sits at the 0.6th percentile of Torn 162-day returns since 2023 ([extra_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/cnc/extra_out.txt)). That decline is statistically unusual, but price data alone cannot attribute it to anything. It ran through the second oil escalation, the opposite of what an oil tracker would do.

The third component is **player share flows**. Torn's public API reports total CNC shares held, about 60.9 billion across 19,962 investors on 27 Nov 2026 ([Tornsy API: stocks](https://tornsy.com/api/stocks)). Daily changes in shares held correlate negatively with CNC's returns: −0.28 on the same day and **−0.75 over 30-day blocks**. Shares rise after down days (corr −0.29), while today's buying does not predict tomorrow's return (−0.02) ([Tornsy API CNC d1](https://tornsy.com/api/cnc?interval=d1&limit=1000)). That lead-lag pattern fits dip-buying better than buying that pushes the price. The motive is CNC's benefit block, a fixed **$80M every 31 days for 7.5M shares** ([Torn Wiki: Stock Market](https://wiki.torn.com/wiki/Stock_Market)), worth about 14–15% a year at current prices. The yield rises as the price falls: in Jul–Sep 2026 it went from 14.19% to 14.99% as CNC slid from 885.17 to 837.60 ([energy_shares_out.txt](file:///C:/Users/Jordach/.claude/jobs/3fcb5b1e/tmp/energy_shares_out.txt)).

The July–September 2026 inflow looks like it could be war-driven but is not. Shares held rose from **27.19B to 64.86B (×2.39)**. The path was lumpy: up to 44.2B on 4 Aug, down 34% to 29.3B by 20 Aug, then up to a peak of 70.3B on 18 Sep. The top five days supplied 72% of the net increase, and single days added billions of shares, which points to a few very large holders. The three biggest days (25, 26 and 29 Aug: +12%, +24% and +18%) fell in the August war lull and match no listed war event. Under the unverified community theory that "purchases … cause downwards movements of price" ([Forum: Guide to Stocks 3.0](https://www.torn.com/forums.php?p=threads&f=61&t=16220358&b=0&a=0&start=0&to=21410794)), buying would push the price down. ## Conclusion

The question was whether CNC "reacts to" or "tracks" the war, and the evidence goes further than a no. CNC lacks even the indirect channels that would let it track the war. Crude, energy equities and the obvious industry analogues for other Torn stocks all fail to predict Torn returns. So the "real-world stocks" link in Torn's 2021 statement either no longer operates at return frequencies or is transformed beyond recognition. The single oil-consistent hour in June 2025 should be treated as a false positive until a future shock repeats it, and the February–March 2026 shock was the best possible test of that and came up empty. Anyone trading CNC on Hormuz headlines is trading noise. The 2026 record actually inverts the oil story: CNC peaked as oil bottomed in late 2025 and posted Torn's worst H2 2026 while Brent returned above $100.

What has predictive content is CNC's own market. Share inflows follow price dips, the fixed $80M payout makes dips self-cushioning through yield-seeking buyers, and long, slow drift regimes are the dominant source of multi-month returns. Three things would sharpen the picture: investor-count and wallet-level flow data to separate whale accumulation from broad participation; a variance-break test at the disputed volatility-update date; and minute-level event studies around the large single-day inflows (26 and 29 Aug 2026) to test directly whether buying moves the price.
