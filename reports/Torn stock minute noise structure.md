# One rule, 35 independent dice: Torn's minute noise

Torn City's per-minute price "fuzz" is **not shared across stocks**. Every stock draws its own noise independently each minute. What the stocks *do* share is the **recipe**: the same proportional scale, the same small negative bounce, and the same game-wide volatility setting, which was cut roughly in half for all 35 stocks at once on **2023-07-12/13**. In the local 1-minute data, cross-stock return correlation is zero within sampling error at every horizon from 1 minute to 1 day. The first principal component explains exactly what pure chance predicts for 35 independent series, and the number of stocks changing price in a given minute follows the independent-coin distribution almost exactly ([noise_cross_stock.py](../tools/noise_cross_stock.py)). Within a single stock, the observed price behaves like a hidden random walk moving about **1 bp (0.01%) per minute**, plus a much smaller transient jitter of **0.1–0.4 bp**, plus rounding to whole cents ([noise_single_stock.py](../tools/noise_single_stock.py)). So "genuine change vs noise" is two separate questions. Almost any move bigger than about **5 jitter-sds (roughly 1.5 bp, or 5 c on a $350 stock)** is real price movement rather than fuzz. But most of that real movement is itself random-walk diffusion, and calling a *trend* takes a CUSUM-style detector working in σ units over tens of minutes to hours. For the bot's `/predict`, the main consequences are that training windows crossing 2023-07-13 mix two volatility regimes, and that thresholds should be set in σ units or percentages, never in cents.

## Shared rules, independent draws: the cross-stock evidence is unambiguous

The decisive test is whether 1-minute returns move together. Over a 90-day window (129,347 fully observed minutes for all 35 stocks), the **mean pairwise correlation of 1-minute log returns is +0.0001**, with a 5th–95th percentile range of −0.0047 to +0.0050. That spread is exactly what 1/√T predicts under independence. The **first principal component explains 2.94% of variance against a Marchenko–Pastur null of 2.95%**. Aggregating to 5, 15, 60, 240 and 1,440 minutes changes nothing: mean ρ stays within ±0.0023, and PC1 tracks the null at every horizon ([noise_cross_stock.py](../tools/noise_cross_stock.py)). Ten 90-day windows from November 2022 to November 2026 all give 1-minute mean ρ between −0.0002 and +0.0001, and a May 2023 window, taken before the regime change, looks the same. Lead-lag correlations at ±1 minute are also zero, so one stock's move does not spill into another a minute later. Cent changes give identical results to log returns, so the null result does not depend on how moves are scaled. The methods literature names two signatures of a shared jitter draw: inflated lag-0 correlation at fine scales, and a negative lag-1 cross-covariance. The data shows neither ([Zhang 2006](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/0411397); [Epps-effect causes, arXiv 1009.6157](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.6157)).

The timing of changes is independent too. On average 27.0 of 35 stocks change price in a given minute, and the variance of that count matches the independent Poisson-binomial prediction (**variance ratio 0.97–1.01**). Among minutes where at least 10 stocks move, the share moving *up* has an sd of 0.0968 against 0.0963 for fair coins. Large jumps (beyond 6 robust sds) essentially never coincide: over 365 days, 33,091 minutes had at least one jump, 1,005 had at least two, 28 had at least three, and none had five or more. That pattern fits chance coincidence of independent per-stock jumps. No minute-of-hour, hour-of-day or 00:00 TCT effect exists in change counts or volatility, and per-stock time-of-day profiles are uncorrelated with each other. The only thing that is genuinely synchronised in the data is **collector downtime**: every stock is missing the same 127 minutes in the last 30 days ([noise_cross_stock.py](../tools/noise_cross_stock.py)). In practice, other stocks carry **no information** about a given stock's next minute, or its next day, and a "market factor" model has nothing to fit. This also matches the community view that "nobody knows the code" and classic technical analysis does not transfer ([Forum: Technical analysis](https://www.torn.com/forums.php?p=threads&f=11&t=16300787&b=0&a=0)).

The shared part is the *distribution*. Noise is **proportional to price**. Across stocks, log(sd in cents) scales with log(price) at a slope of 0.98, while the percentage sd is flat (slope −0.02). The coefficient of variation across stocks is 6–9% for percentage sd against 61% for cent sd, and the median stock has sat at **0.0111–0.0117% per minute in every month from August 2023 to November 2026** ([noise_cross_stock.py](../tools/noise_cross_stock.py)). Every stock in every period shows negative lag-1 autocorrelation (monthly median −0.045 to −0.081 since 2021). That is one generating mechanism, a "hidden price + transient fuzz" rule, applied stock by stock with separate random draws. Cheap stocks only look noisier in a different way because of the 1-cent tick. yaz at $53 has 59% zero-change minutes and 90% of its non-zero moves are exactly 1 c, while tsb at $1,164 has 14% zeros and a median move of 7–8 c.

## Inside one stock: a 1 bp/min random walk wearing 0.3 bp of fuzz

The minute-to-minute change is dominated by the hidden price's own motion, not by fuzz. In the textbook random-walk-plus-noise model, 1-minute returns are MA(1). Their lag-1 autocovariance equals −σe², and the variance signature Var(k)/k falls from k = 1 to a plateau at σu² ([Roll 1984](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/roll1984.pdf); [Hansen & Lunde](https://web-docs.stern.nyu.edu/salomon/docs/Hansen-Lunde.pdf)). Torn data fits that picture closely. Lag-1 autocorrelation is −0.02 to −0.10 (yaz −0.18) and dies within 2–3 lags. The signature plateaus by k ≈ 20–30 minutes and stays flat to about ±10% out to 4 hours. Maximum-likelihood Kalman fits over 30 days give **σu ≈ 0.96–1.27 bp/min** for every stock tested, and **σe ≈ 0.1–0.4 bp**. The signal-to-noise ratio q = σu²/σe² is therefore about 8–20 (fhg reaches 150), and the steady-state Kalman gain is **K ≈ 0.90–0.96** ([noise_single_stock.py](../tools/noise_single_stock.py)). In plain terms, jitter is only about 5–15% of one-minute variance, and the best estimate of the "true" price is almost the latest print. For yaz, most of the estimated noise is cent rounding: genuine jitter is about 0.14 c. For ass the rounding floor is negligible and the roughly 1.1 c of jitter is real.

| yaz | $52.85 | 0.74 c (1.40 bp) | 0.61 bp* | 1.11 bp | 0.81 | ~1.5 c* |
| ass | $351.6 | 3.90 c (1.11 bp) | 0.32 bp (1.13 c) | 1.01 bp | 0.92 | ~4.9–5.7 c |

The fuzz is not a neat bounded uniform jitter. A "difference of two uniform draws" (triangular) model of 1-minute changes is decisively rejected. Student-t or Laplace fits beat a Gaussian, the largest move grows with sample size, and there is no hard ±k cliff. Rolling-median residuals grow like √window, which is the signature of an underlying diffusion, not of a fixed noise band ([noise_single_stock.py](../tools/noise_single_stock.py)). Two oddities stand out. Zero-change minutes are far more common than a smooth distribution allows, and they **cluster** (cnc: P(zero | previous zero) = 0.55 against 0.29 unconditionally), but they are not missed samples, because the minute after a zero is not larger than usual. Expensive stocks also show a stock-specific "comb" of preferred cent sizes (cnc peaks at ±6–7 c, fhg at ±2–3 c) that is neither a fixed bp nor a fixed cent unit. Its mechanism is unknown. Jitter also has a short memory of about 1–3 minutes rather than being strictly i.i.d. These details mean a Gaussian model under-predicts both zeros and large moves. Real data therefore produces 2–5× the false-alarm rate of simulated data, and robust or volatility-adaptive scaling is worth using.

## Telling genuine moves from fuzz takes three different rules

The analysis supports three detectors, all expressed in σ units so they carry across stocks and regimes. The **level rule** asks whether a move is fuzz. When the Kalman-filtered price leaves its anchor by more than **5σe**, that produced **zero false alarms in 30 simulated days of pure noise** for every stock tested, while 4σe produced 0–4.2 per day and 3σe 17–76 per day. On real data, 5σe fires 117–741 times per day, because the hidden price really does diffuse that far every few minutes. Almost every move larger than about 1.5 bp is genuine price movement and not fuzz ([noise_single_stock.py](../tools/noise_single_stock.py)). The **jump rule** flags a single-minute innovation with |z| > 6. Under the Gaussian model that happens about 0 times per day, yet real data shows 0.03 per day on ass and 2.7 per day on fhg. These are genuine one-minute jumps, and they concentrate in particular stocks (ist, fhg, evl) rather than particular minutes. A cheap manual check follows from the noise model. A noise spike reverses the next minute (r_{t+1} ≈ −r_t), while a genuine shift holds its new level ([jump-test survey, arXiv 1708.09520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.09520)). Do not run raw Lee-Mykland or bipower jump tests on the jittered 1-minute series without pre-averaging, because negative ρ1 biases them.

The hard question is whether a stock is *trending* or just random-walking. With σu ≈ 1.05 bp/min, pure diffusion alone gives an sd of about **8 bp (0.08%) over an hour and about 40 bp (0.4%) over a day**. That figure is derived from the measured signature plateau of about 1.0–1.2 bp² per minute. Moves of that size are what "nothing is happening" looks like. The **trend rule** is a two-sided CUSUM on standardised Kalman innovations with **k = 0.125 and h = 25**. It gives 0.13 false alarms per day under the zero-drift model and 0.23–0.60 per day on real data. It detects a drift of 0.5σu per minute (about 0.3% per hour) with a median delay of **56 minutes**, and a drift of 0.25σu per minute (about 0.15% per hour) in **143–160 minutes**, with 82–84% caught within 4 hours. For strong drifts of at least 1σu per minute, k = 0.5 with h = 10 is faster (about 17 minutes). On fat-tailed stocks such as fhg, adding volatility-adaptive scaling (`--adapt-hl 120`) cut real-data alarms from 0.60 to 0.33 per day ([noise_single_stock.py](../tools/noise_single_stock.py)). Do not expect momentum from these alarms. The 60-minute follow-through after an alarm averaged only +0.5 to +5.4 bp with hit rates of 0.50–0.64 on samples of 11–40, which is consistent with a hidden price that is close to a pure random walk. A centred rolling median, the intuitive denoiser, is the worst option here: its 15–61-minute residuals are 3–8× the jitter, so it smears genuine movement and adds lag.

## The volatility cut came in July 2023, whatever the forum snippet says

The data shows one sharp, game-wide regime change. Median per-stock 1-minute sd was **0.020–0.024% through 2023-07-11**, fell to 0.017% on 07-12, then to **0.0027% on 07-13** and 0.0040% on 07-14. The number of stocks below 0.016% daily sd jumped from 0–4 to **35 of 35** on 07-13. Volatility then oscillated back up over about two weeks and settled at about 0.011% from late July, where it has stayed ever since ([noise_cross_stock.py](../tools/noise_cross_stock.py)). Quarterly figures for ass, fhg, yaz, tsb and sys agree: 2.1–3.3 bp per minute in 2022 (ass 4.54 in 2023Q2) against 1.04–1.45 bp from 2023Q3 onward. Earlier history shows a further level of about 0.055% in April–June 2021, which fell to about 0.025% from September 2021 ([noise_single_stock.py](../tools/noise_single_stock.py)).

The only staff statement found is the "Stock Market - Volatility Update". Search snippets date it to **November 2023**. It says the engine change would reduce "very short-term rebounding", dampen short-term swings without changing the direction of moves, let volatility "come back over a few days", leave mid- and long-term trends "mostly unaffected", and correct a roughly $60 TCSE drift ([Forum: Stock Market - Volatility Update](https://www.torn.com/forums.php?p=threads&t=16341610)). The thread returned HTTP 403, so neither its date nor its wording could be checked directly. The best reconciliation is that **the announcement and the July 2023 break are the same event, and the "November 2023" date is a snippet artefact**. A search engine can easily date a thread with roughly 800 replies by a late reply rather than the original post. Three points support this. The data shows **no change at all in November 2023**: median sd sits at 0.0111–0.0117% in every month from August 2023. The July event matches the announced behaviour exactly, with a market-wide dampening followed by a recovery of volatility over days. And no other break of comparable size exists anywhere in 2023–2026. The alternative, steelmanned, is that staff made a second, subtler change in November 2023 that targeted only bounce and was invisible in total volatility. The evidence argues against that too. Lag-1 autocorrelation, the direct measure of "rebounding", has stayed between −0.045 and −0.081 throughout with no break in 2023. And the July 2023 cut scaled jitter and diffusion down together: Roll jitter went from 0.0066% to about 0.003% while q stayed roughly constant, which suggests a single global volatility multiplier rather than a targeted anti-bounce fix. The way to settle it is to read the thread's first post while logged in to Torn.

Two data caveats apply. Local data has **35 stocks** (ass … yaz), consistent with the 35 stocks listed by community tools ([Kokomo Exchange](https://kokomo-data.kokomoexchange.workers.dev/); [Torn Stock Analyzer](https://greasyfork.org/scripts/570460-torn-stock-analyzer)). Coverage also dips badly between about May 2024 and June 2025 (11–20% of minutes missing), but the gaps are shared across all stocks and both scripts never difference across a missing minute.

## Using the two scripts

Both scripts open `db/db_<ticker>.db` read-only, place prices on a regular 1-minute UTC grid, and drop any difference that touches a missing minute. Both default to the repo's `db/` and fall back to the main checkout's `db/` when run from a git worktree; pass `--db-dir` to point anywhere else. Note the different ticker syntax: the cross-stock script takes a **comma-separated** list, and the single-stock script takes a **space-separated** list or `all`.

```bash
DB=db   # or the main checkout's db/ folder

# Shared vs independent? correlations, PCA vs MP null, co-change counts, scaling, Roll, seasonality
python tools/noise_cross_stock.py --db-dir $DB --days 30                 # ~5 s, all 35 stocks
python tools/noise_cross_stock.py --db-dir $DB --days 90 --horizons 1,5,15,60,240,1440 \
       --stability 10 --out reports/cross90.md                           # ~1-2 min; .csv = per-stock table
python tools/noise_cross_stock.py --db-dir $DB --end 2023-05-30 --days 30 --tickers ass,tcp,fhg

# Per-stock: distribution, ACF, signature, Kalman MLE (sigma_e, sigma_u, q, K)
python tools/noise_single_stock.py characterise --db-dir $DB --tickers ass fhg yaz --days 30 --out out/
# Per-minute latent price + detector flags to out/filter_<ticker>.csv
python tools/noise_single_stock.py filter --db-dir $DB --tickers ass --days 7 --out out/
# Calibrate: false alarms on simulated noise, delays for injected steps/trends
python tools/noise_single_stock.py evaluate --db-dir $DB --tickers ass --days 30 --kref 0.125 --h 25
```

`noise_cross_stock.py` ([source](../tools/noise_cross_stock.py)) needs numpy and pandas, with scipy optional. Its flags are `--days` (default 30), `--end` (UTC date, default latest data), `--tickers`, `--horizons` (default `1,5,15,60`), `--stability N` (repeats the headline metrics in N windows spread across history), `--jump-z` (default 6, robust z) and `--out` (`.md` for the full report, `.csv` for the per-stock table). It also exports reusable loaders: `load_m1(db_dir, ticker, start_ts, end_ts)`, `list_tickers(db_dir)` and `load_panel(...)`. `noise_single_stock.py` ([source](../tools/noise_single_stock.py)) needs scipy and works in bp of log price. Its subcommands are `characterise`, `filter` and `evaluate`. The detector flags default to the recommended settings: `--kref 0.125`, `--h 25`, `--band 5` (in σe), `--jump-z 6`, plus an optional `--adapt-hl` (EWMA half-life in minutes for vol-adaptive CUSUM) and `--median-window 15`. `evaluate` also takes `--sim-days`, `--h-grid`, `--band-grid`, `--step-grid`, `--trend-grid`, `--n-inject`, `--horizon` and `--seed`. The `filter` CSV columns are `date, utc, price, kalman, kalman_sd_cents, ewma, median_trailing, innov_z, cusum_alarm, level_alarm, jump_flag`, where the alarm columns hold ±1 for direction. Treat fitted σe as window-dependent: a 7-day fhg window gave 7× the 30-day σe². Re-fit on 30–90 days before trusting the thresholds.

## What this means for `/predict`

`/predict` on the `gold` branch (`tsl_core/analysis/predict.py`) already does most of what the noise structure calls for: causal, price-level-free features; log-return targets from direct XGBoost models; walk-forward `TimeSeriesSplit` skill measured against "no change" and "drift" baselines; and a conformal 80% band. The noise findings back those choices up and add three points. First, **regime mixing** is the one real gap. `/predict` offers m30 to d1 with 2,000 bars by default, capped at 16,000. At the default, d1 (the whole history back to 2021) and h12 (about 2.7 years) reach back before 2023-07-13, and so does h2 and up once `samples` is raised. Those fits blend a regime with roughly twice today's per-minute volatility into the current one. Log-return targets and the realised-volatility ratio feature soften this, but the quantile band and its conformal calibration still see the wider old-regime spread. Truncate training rows at 2023-07-13, or divide returns by a per-regime σ. Second, **the baseline is right**: the hidden price is close to a random walk, so "no change" with an error band of about σu·√(minutes ahead) (roughly 0.08% per hour and 0.4% per day today) is the honest yardstick, which is what the skill metric already uses. Third, **thresholds** should be expressed in σ units or percentages, never cents. Pre-smoothing prices before fitting buys little (K ≈ 0.9 removes only 1–3 c at m1), and cross-stock features will not help, because the stocks are independent.

## Conclusion

The working model of a Torn stock is now concrete. Each stock is its own multiplicative random walk at about 1 bp per minute, with fat tails, clustered volatility, intermittent quiet spells and occasional jumps, wrapped in a thin layer of short-memory jitter and cent rounding. All 35 stocks are driven by one global volatility dial and never by a shared draw. The practical shift for anyone building signals is that "noise" in the fuzz sense is nearly solved: anything beyond about 5σe is real. The open problem is the random walk itself. Most real movement is unpredictable diffusion, and detection effort is better spent on CUSUM-style trend confirmation and jump flagging than on denoising.

Three open questions are worth pursuing. One is confirming the date and content of the staff volatility thread directly. Another is the mechanism behind the per-stock cent "comb" and the clustered zero-change minutes, since these are the only structure not yet explained and could reflect a hidden higher-precision price or a discrete step menu in the engine. The third is a regime-switching (quiet / normal / jump) filter that would replace the Gaussian approximation and cut the 2–5× excess false-alarm rate seen on real data.
